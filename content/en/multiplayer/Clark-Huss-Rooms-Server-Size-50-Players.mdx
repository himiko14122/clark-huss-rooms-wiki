## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does Clark Huss rooms use 50-player servers?**

The developer chose 50-player servers to create a more chaotic, unpredictable environment. The design philosophy prioritizes tension over coordination ease.

**Is 50-player server better or worse than smaller servers?**

It depends on your playstyle. Coordinated squads prefer partial servers. Players who enjoy chaos prefer full lobbies.

**How do I find partial servers?**

Use server browser tools or join during off-peak hours (early morning, late night). Weekday afternoons are typically busiest.

**Does Clark behave differently in full lobbies?**

Yes. Clark's targeting shifts based on player distribution and audio signature density. Full lobbies have more variables affecting his behavior.

**Should I always play in partial servers?**

For serious runs, yes. For chaotic fun or learning, full lobbies are fine. Choose based on your goals.

For more on coordination in 50-player servers, see our [co-op survival guide](/en/multiplayer/co-op-survival-guide/). To learn the communication tactics that work in noisy environments, read [communication tactics](/en/multiplayer/communication-tactics/).


**How do I handle toxic squadmates?**

Mute toxic players immediately. Focus on coordinating with positive squadmates. If the entire squad is toxic, leave the lobby and find better players. Long-term squad building through community channels reduces exposure to random toxic behavior.

**What if my squad disagrees on strategy?**

Pre-game strategy agreement prevents mid-run conflicts. If disagreements arise during play, defer to the assigned Lead. Post-run discussions can address strategy issues constructively. Avoid changing strategies mid-run unless facing immediate failure.

**How do I improve as a squad over time?**

Regular play together builds coordination habits. Review failed runs to identify improvement areas. Practice specific scenarios like chases or battery management. Rotate roles to develop well-rounded squad members. Track progress over multiple sessions.

**Should I use Roblox voice chat or Discord?**

Discord generally provides better audio quality and more features than Roblox voice chat. Discord supports push-to-talk, noise suppression, and channel separation. Roblox voice is convenient but less reliable. Most coordinated squads prefer Discord.

**What is the best way to communicate in chaotic lobbies?**

Use standardized callouts and avoid extended discussions. Push-to-talk discipline prevents audio clutter. Calm voice designation helps stabilize communication. Mute chaotic players to focus on your squad.

**Can I play with friends across different time zones?**

Time zone differences require scheduling flexibility. Some squads coordinate across multiple time zones by playing at overlapping times. Others form regional sub-squads. Find what works for your specific situation.

**How do I build trust with new squadmates?**

Trust builds through consistent play and reliable performance. Show up when scheduled, perform your role, and communicate clearly. Avoid drama and focus on gameplay. Trust develops naturally over multiple sessions.


**What is the most common mistake when starting out?**

The most common mistake is treating Clark Huss rooms like a typical horror game where visual cues dominate. Audio-first design means new players must develop listening skills that other games do not require. This learning curve catches many experienced horror game players who rely on visual instincts they have built elsewhere.

**How do experienced players differ from beginners?**

Experienced players move more deliberately, use flashlight less frequently, communicate more clearly in squads, and recognize Clark audio tells within fractions of a second. Beginners tend to sprint, hold flashlight continuously, panic during chases, and miss critical audio cues. The gap closes with deliberate practice over 5 — 10 sessions.

**What is the biggest improvement you can make quickly?**

Improving flashlight discipline provides the fastest improvement for most players. Switching from continuous flashlight use to click-only mode extends battery life dramatically. The extended battery life means more visual information is available when needed, improving overall performance.

**How important is audio equipment really?**

Audio equipment is critical. Built-in laptop speakers and TV speakers cannot provide the directional audio cues the game is designed around. A $30 pair of wired earbuds dramatically improves gameplay compared to built-in speakers. The investment pays off immediately.

**Should I focus on solo or squad play?**

Focus on the mode that matches your goals. Solo play builds individual skills faster. Squad play provides longer survival times and social engagement. Most veterans play both modes to develop well-rounded abilities. Choose based on your priorities and available time.

**What role does luck play in survival?**

Luck affects run outcomes significantly because of procedural generation. A well-prepared player in an unlucky layout may die, while a less prepared player in a favorable layout may survive. Focus on skill development to minimize the role of luck over time. Long-term success requires consistency across many runs.

**How do I avoid burnout?**

Burnout is real for active players. Take breaks between sessions, play other games, and vary your squadmates. Set realistic goals for each session. If you find yourself frustrated, step away for a day or two. Returning fresh improves both enjoyment and performance.

**What is the best way to learn advanced techniques?**

Study veteran gameplay through community resources. Watch experienced players on streaming platforms. Read detailed guides on specific topics. Practice specific techniques in isolation before applying them in real runs. Deliberate learning accelerates skill development compared to passive gameplay.
