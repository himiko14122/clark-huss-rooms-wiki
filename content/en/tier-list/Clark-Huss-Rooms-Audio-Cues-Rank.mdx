Not all audio cues in Clark Huss rooms are equally important for survival. Some cues provide early warning that lets you avoid danger entirely, while others only confirm danger that has already arrived. Learning the cues in the correct priority order dramatically shortens your learning curve. New players often learn low-priority cues first because they are easier to identify, then struggle to learn the high-priority cues that actually determine survival outcomes.

This tier list ranks the audio cues from S (highest survival value) to D (lowest priority), explains the information each cue provides, and recommends the optimal learning order. You will also learn how cue importance changes based on run phase and squad composition.

## Audio Cue Importance Framework

Each audio cue is rated on a four-tier scale based on three factors: information value, actionability, and reliability. The factors determine how much the cue contributes to survival decisions.

### **Cue importance factors:**

| Factor | Weight | Description |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Information value | 40% | How much data the cue provides about Clark's state |
| Actionability | 40% | Whether the cue triggers a clear survival response |
| Reliability | 20% | How consistently the cue indicates its intended meaning |

The combined weighting produces a single tier ranking. The ranking below uses this framework to prioritize cue learning.

## Tier S: Critical Survival Cues

Tier S cues provide immediate, actionable information that directly determines survival outcomes. These cues must be learned first and recognized within one second of hearing them.

### **Tier S cues:**

| Cue | Information | Action | Learning Priority |
|-----|-------------|--------|-------------------|
| Sprint burst | Clark in active chase | Full sprint, flashlight off | First |
| Breathing cadence (rapid) | Clark tracking, close | Controlled retreat | First |
| Whisper fragments | Clark elevated alertness | Evasion preparation | Second |
| Heartbeat (racing) | Clark in pursuit | Identify escape route | Second |

These four cues form the core survival skill set. New players who master these four cues survive runs that would otherwise end in the first three minutes.

## Detection Priority Conflicts

When multiple cues play simultaneously, your brain must prioritize which to focus on. The detection priority order below resolves conflicts.

**Priority order during multi-cue situations:**

1. Sprint burst (always highest)
2. Whisper fragments (chase warning)
3. Rapid breathing (close tracking)
4. Normal breathing (close patrolling)
5. Footstep thuds (adjacent room)
6. Heartbeat variations (distant awareness)
7. Environmental cues (navigation)

When two cues conflict (e.g., distant heartbeat and close breathing), always prioritize the closer cue because it represents more immediate danger.

## Cue Reliability Notes

Some cues are more reliable than others because their trigger conditions are consistent. The reliability rating helps you interpret ambiguous cues.

**Cue reliability ratings:**

| Cue | Reliability | Notes |
|-----|-------------|-------|
| Sprint burst | Very high | Always indicates chase |
| Whisper fragments | High | Usually indicates elevated alertness |
| Breathing cadence | High | Reliable but can be confused with own breathing |
| Heartbeat | Very high | Consistent volume-distance relationship |
| Footstep thuds | Medium | Can be confused with environmental thuds |
| Door lock click | Very high | Distinctive sound |
| Air vent hum | High | Consistent across rooms |

New players should focus on high-reliability cues first because the interpretation is unambiguous. Lower-reliability cues require more experience to interpret correctly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which cue should I learn first?**

Sprint burst is the highest priority because it triggers the most critical response. Learn to recognize sprint burst within one week of starting.

**How long does it take to learn all cues?**

Veteran estimates range from six weeks for the core cues to three months for all cues including decorative ones. The timeline depends on play frequency.

**Can I learn cues faster with community tools?**

Yes. Audio training tools can compress the learning timeline by 30 — 40%. The tools isolate cues for practice, which is faster than in-game learning.

**Do cues change between patches?**

Sometimes. Early Access patches may adjust cue volumes or add new variants. Check the patch tracker regularly for cue-related changes.

**What if I cannot distinguish Clark's breathing from my own?**

This is a common confusion for new players. The volume test helps: if you hear the breathing equally in both ears, it is your own. If it is directional, it is Clark.

**How often should I re-evaluate tier rankings?**

Tier rankings should be re-evaluated after major patches and quarterly during stable periods. Strategy effectiveness shifts with game changes. Veterans track tier rankings over time and adjust their playstyle accordingly. Personal tier rankings may differ from community consensus.

**Are tier rankings universal or platform-specific?**

Tier rankings have platform-specific variations. PC players have advantages in audio-dependent strategies. Mobile players may prefer different approaches due to control and audio quality differences. Consider platform when applying tier rankings to your play.

**How do I create my own tier rankings?**

Track your own performance with different strategies across multiple runs. Note success rates, survival times, and enjoyment. Combine personal data with community consensus to form your own rankings. Update rankings as you gain more experience and the game evolves.

**Should tier rankings influence my strategy choice?**

Tier rankings inform strategy choice but should not dictate it. Personal preference and playstyle matter alongside effectiveness. A slightly weaker strategy that matches your strengths may outperform a stronger strategy that does not fit your playstyle. Balance rankings with personal factors.

**What is the difference between tier rankings and meta?**

Tier rankings evaluate individual strategies in isolation. Meta describes the overall strategic landscape including dominant combinations, counter-strategies, and balance shifts. Both are useful for understanding the game state. Meta analysis often references tier rankings as components.

**How do squad strategies affect tier rankings?**

Squad strategies often rank higher than solo strategies because coordination multiplies effectiveness. Solo strategies rank higher in solo play contexts. When evaluating tier rankings, consider whether the strategy is intended for solo or squad play. Some strategies only work in one context.

**Can tier rankings be wrong?**

Tier rankings are community consensus based on observation, not absolute truth. Individual experiences may differ from consensus. Some strategies that rank lower may work better for specific players. Use tier rankings as guidance, not absolute rules.
