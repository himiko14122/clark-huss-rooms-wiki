Clark Huss rooms generates corridors from a fixed pattern library of about twelve distinct types, and each room type has unique navigation characteristics, safety profiles, and tactical uses. Understanding which room types are safe, which are dangerous, and which are tactically valuable is essential for spatial mapping and strategic planning. New players treat all corridors the same, which causes them to enter dangerous rooms unprepared.

This tier list ranks the twelve room types from S (most tactically valuable) to F (least useful), explains the navigation characteristics of each, and provides recommendations for which room types to prioritize during different run phases. You will also learn how to identify room types quickly through audio and flashlight click patterns.

## Room Type Tier Framework

Each room type is ranked on a five-tier scale based on three factors: navigation value, safety during Clark encounters, and tactical utility. The tiers help you prioritize which rooms to enter first and which to avoid.

### **Room type tier factors:**

| Factor | Weight | Description |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Navigation value | 40% | How well the room helps with spatial mapping |
| Safety during Clark encounters | 40% | How vulnerable the room is to Clark detection |
| Tactical utility | 20% | Strategic value for escape or regrouping |

The combined weighting produces a single tier ranking from S to F. The tier list below uses this framework to rank all twelve room types.

## Tier S: Strategic Open Rooms

Tier S rooms are large open spaces that provide tactical value for spatial mapping, escape routes, and squad regrouping. These rooms are the safest places to pause during chases.

### **Tier S room types:**

| Room Type | Characteristics | Best Use |
|-----------|-----------------|----------|
| Hub room | Large central area with four or more doorways | Map checkpoint, squad regrouping |
| Staircase | Multi-level access with visible structure | Navigation landmark, escape route |
| Echo chamber | Large room with distinctive reverb | Audio landmark, audio cue training |

### **Tier S room tactical advantages:**

- Multiple exit options for escape
- Audio landmarks for navigation
- Space for squad formation maneuvers
- Visual landmarks for spatial memory

These rooms are rare because the procedural generator limits their frequency. When you find one, mark it mentally as a return point.

## Tier A: Long Corridor Junctions

Tier A rooms are junction points that connect multiple corridors. They are valuable for navigation because they allow route selection and they provide corner break options during chases.

### **Tier A room types:**

| Room Type | Characteristics | Best Use |
|-----------|-----------------|----------|
| Cross junction | Four-way intersection | Route selection, escape option |
| T-junction | Three-way intersection | Route selection |
| L-junction | Two-way corner | Corner break, escape route |

**Tier A room tactical advantages:**

- Multiple direction options
- Corner break geometry for chases
- Predictable audio behavior
- Frequent generation (good for mapping)

These are the most common room types and form the backbone of your mental map. Veterans identify these rooms within one second of entering them.

## Tier B: Standard Yellow Halls

Tier B rooms are the standard corridors that make up most of the procedurally generated layout. They are safe to navigate but provide limited tactical value beyond basic connectivity.

**Tier B room types:**

| Room Type | Characteristics | Best Use |
|-----------|-----------------|----------|
| Long straight hall | 30+ meters, single direction | Stamina recovery, flashlight conservation |
| Standard yellow corridor | 10 — 30 meters, single direction | Standard navigation |
| Curved corridor | Non-straight path, single direction | Visual variety, audio landmark |

**Tier B room tactical considerations:**

- Long straight halls can be death traps if Clark chases from behind
- Curved corridors break audio line-of-sight, useful for evasion
- Standard yellow corridors are the bread-and-butter of navigation

These rooms fill the procedural layout. You spend most of your time in Tier B rooms, which makes navigation discipline important.

## Tier C: Ambiguous Spaces

Tier C rooms have uncertain tactical value because their safety and navigation properties depend on specific conditions. They require careful evaluation before committing to them.

**Tier C room types:**

| Room Type | Characteristics | Best Use |
|-----------|-----------------|----------|
| Wide passage room | Single direction, wider than standard | Unknown, evaluate on entry |
| Junction with locked door | Three-way with one locked exit | Conditional route selection |
| Partially open room | Has structural obstruction | Defensive position |

**Tier C room evaluation triggers:**

- Audio behavior differs from standard
- Visual layout includes unusual features
- Locked door indicators are visible
- Squad separation risk is elevated

These rooms require active assessment rather than automatic response. Veterans spend five to ten seconds evaluating Tier C rooms before committing.

## Tier D: Risky Passageways

Tier D rooms carry elevated risk because they have specific danger characteristics. Entering them without preparation can lead to Clark encounters with poor escape options.

**Tier D room types:**

| Room Type | Characteristics | Best Use |
|-----------|-----------------|----------|
| Narrow corridor | Tight passage, limited movement | Last resort, not preferred |
| L-shaped with one exit | Two segments, one return point | Use only if no other option |
| T-junction with audio anomaly | Three-way with echo behavior | Investigate with audio first |

**Tier D room risks:**

- Limited escape options during chases
- Audio behavior may differ from standard
- Clark can corner you more easily
- Squad formation maintenance is harder

These rooms should be entered carefully and only when the navigation path requires it. New players often enter Tier D rooms reflexively and regret it.

## Tier F: Avoidance Zones

Tier F rooms should be avoided when possible because they provide minimal tactical value and elevated danger. They are sometimes necessary to enter, but never preferred.

**Tier F room types:**

| Room Type | Characteristics | Best Use |
|-----------|-----------------|----------|
| Dead end | No exit except return | Backtrack immediately |
| Corridor with closed door | Door blocks further passage | Turn around |
| Single direction with obstacle | Path blocked | Find alternative route |

**Tier F room recognition:**

- No second exit visible
- Door indicators show locked
- Audio behavior suggests enclosed space
- Visual cues indicate blockage

Recognizing Tier F rooms before entering them saves time. New players walk into dead ends thinking they are loops, then must backtrack while Clark closes in.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the rarest room type in Clark Huss rooms?**

Dead ends are the rarest at roughly 0.2% generation frequency. When you find one, it is genuinely unusual.

**Which room type is safest during a Clark chase?**

Curved corridors and L-junctions are best because they break audio line-of-sight. Long straight halls are worst because Clark can chase straight through.

**Can I identify room types without flashlight?**

Yes, audio identification works without flashlight. Veterans rely on audio during low-battery phases.

**Should I prioritize Tier S rooms when I find them?**

Yes, but only briefly. Tier S rooms are valuable for regrouping and spatial mapping, not for extended stay. Spend 30 seconds and move on.

The tier list may include new room types as the developer expands the procedural generation. The framework is designed to accommodate additions.
