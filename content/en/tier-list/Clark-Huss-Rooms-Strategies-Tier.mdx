Clark Huss rooms has multiple viable survival strategies, and the best strategy depends on your platform, squad size, and personal playstyle. New players often default to panic sprinting because it feels productive, but the data consistently shows that controlled strategies outperform panic strategies by a wide margin. This tier list ranks the major survival strategies from S (most effective) to C (least reliable), explains the tradeoffs of each approach, and recommends which strategies to use for specific situations.

The tier list reflects the Early Access meta as of July 2026 and will evolve as patches adjust Clark's behavior and balance.

## Strategy Tier Framework

Each strategy is rated on a four-tier scale based on three factors: survival effectiveness, learning curve, and consistency. The factors determine overall viability for different player types.

### **Strategy tier factors:**

| Factor | Weight | Description |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Survival effectiveness | 50% | How often the strategy leads to successful runs |
| Learning curve | 30% | How quickly new players can adopt the strategy |
| Consistency | 20% | How reliably the strategy works across different situations |

The combined weighting produces the final tier ranking. Strategies that require extensive practice but provide high effectiveness can rank highly despite the learning curve.

## Tier S: Controlled Retreat Strategy

Controlled retreat is the highest-ranked strategy because it balances effectiveness, learning curve, and consistency. Veterans consistently achieve longer runs with this strategy than any other.

### **Controlled retreat characteristics:**

| Aspect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Movement pace | Walking only, occasional sprint for escape |
| Flashlight use | Click-only mode, conservative battery |
| Audio focus | Continuous monitoring, decision-driven |
| Risk profile | Low risk, slow progression |
| Best squad size | Any (solo to full lobby) |

### **Controlled retreat execution sequence:**

1. Walk at sustained pace through corridors
2. Click flashlight once per junction decision
3. Listen for Clark audio tells at every step
4. Pause briefly when heartbeat changes
5. Adjust pace based on Clark's behavioral tier
6. Sprint only when Tier S threat is active

The strategy's effectiveness comes from reducing Clark's detection opportunities while maintaining progress.

## Tier A: Audio-First Movement Strategy

Audio-first movement is the second-ranked strategy and shares many characteristics with controlled retreat. The difference is the explicit focus on audio as the primary information source.

### **Audio-first characteristics:**

| Aspect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Movement pace | Variable, adjusted to audio conditions |
| Flashlight use | Minimal, audio preferred |
| Audio focus | Continuous, pre-emptive listening |
| Risk profile | Medium risk, requires audio mastery |
| Best squad size | Duo to quatro |

**Audio-first execution:**

1. Stop at every junction and listen for 2 — 3 seconds before proceeding
2. Use audio cues to determine if Clark is nearby
3. Click flashlight only when entering unexplored areas
4. Adjust route based on audio information
5. Communicate audio findings to squad (multiplayer)

The strategy requires strong audio interpretation skills. Players who have completed the audio training sequence will find it highly effective.

## Tier B: Silent Run Strategy

Silent run is the third-ranked strategy and focuses on minimum sound profile at all times. The strategy is effective but slow, which limits its use to specific situations.

**Silent run characteristics:**

| Aspect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Movement pace | Slow walking, no sprint |
| Flashlight use | Off entirely when possible |
| Audio focus | Passive listening, no active scanning |
| Risk profile | Low risk, very slow progression |
| Best squad size | Solo only |

**Silent run execution:**

1. Move at the slowest pace that maintains progress
2. Flashlight off unless absolutely necessary
3. Listen passively, do not actively scan
4. Wait for Clark to move away before continuing
5. Use environmental audio for navigation

Silent run works best for solo players who have time to invest. In multiplayer, the slow pace frustrates squadmates and creates pressure to speed up.

## Tier B: Squad Coordination Strategy

Squad coordination is tied with silent run at Tier B because its effectiveness depends heavily on squad quality. A well-coordinated squad outperforms uncoordinated ones by a wide margin, but achieving coordination requires investment.

**Squad coordination characteristics:**

| Aspect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Movement pace | Variable, controlled by Lead |
| Flashlight use | Coordinated, Light Manager controls |
| Audio focus | Distributed across roles |
| Risk profile | Low risk if coordinated, high if not |
| Best squad size | Trio to quatro (sweet spot) |

**Squad coordination execution:**

1. Assign Lead, Rear Guard, Light Manager before server lock
2. Use standardized callout vocabulary
3. Move in formation (wedge or single file)
4. Light Manager controls all flashlight clicks
5. Rear Guard monitors Clark audio and calls out threats

The strategy requires pre-game agreement and rehearsal. Random public lobbies cannot execute it effectively.

## Tier C: Panic Sprint Strategy

Panic sprint is the lowest-ranked strategy because it feels productive but consistently fails. It is included in the tier list because new players default to it and need to understand why it does not work.

**Panic sprint characteristics:**

| Aspect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Movement pace | Continuous sprint |
| Flashlight use | Spam clicking, often sustained |
| Audio focus | None, overwhelmed by own audio |
| Risk profile | Very high, guaranteed Clark detection |
| Best squad size | None |

**Why panic sprint fails:**

| Reason | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Sprint audio is loud | Clark tracks directly to player |
| Sprint burns stamina | No reserves for later escape |
| Flashlight spam reveals position | Through walls, double exposure |
| No audio monitoring | Cannot hear Clark's approach |
| Predictable direction | Clark can intercept easily |

Panic sprint is the strategy that new players use instinctively when they hear Clark. Veterans recognize the panic spiral and interrupt it with controlled retreat before it starts.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best strategy for a brand new player?**

Controlled retreat is the best starting strategy because it has the lowest skill floor and the highest consistency. New players should default to controlled retreat until they have mastered the basics.

**Which strategy has the highest skill ceiling?**

Squad coordination has the highest skill ceiling because it requires multiple players executing in sync. A perfectly coordinated squad outperforms any solo strategy by 30 — 50% in survival time.

**Should I switch strategies mid-run?**

Sometimes. If your current strategy is failing (repeated Clark encounters, low battery, low stamina), switching to controlled retreat is a safe fallback. Avoid switching to panic sprint as a "desperation move."

**Do strategies differ between PC and mobile?**

Yes. Audio-first movement is harder on mobile because of audio quality variations. Mobile players should default to controlled retreat or silent run.

**How does the meta evolve after patches?**

After each patch, community consensus shifts. The tier list is updated based on observed run outcomes over the first week after a patch.

Tier rankings have platform-specific variations. PC players have advantages in audio-dependent strategies. Mobile players may prefer different approaches due to control and audio quality differences. Consider platform when applying tier rankings to your play.

Track your own performance with different strategies across multiple runs. Note success rates, survival times, and enjoyment. Combine personal data with community consensus to form your own rankings. Update rankings as you gain more experience and the game evolves.

Tier rankings inform strategy choice but should not dictate it. Personal preference and playstyle matter alongside effectiveness. A slightly weaker strategy that matches your strengths may outperform a stronger strategy that does not fit your playstyle. Balance rankings with personal factors.

**What is the difference between tier rankings and meta?**

Tier rankings evaluate individual strategies in isolation. Meta describes the overall strategic landscape including dominant combinations, counter-strategies, and balance shifts. Both are useful for understanding the game state. Meta analysis often references tier rankings as components.

**How do squad strategies affect tier rankings?**

Squad strategies often rank higher than solo strategies because coordination multiplies effectiveness. Solo strategies rank higher in solo play contexts. When evaluating tier rankings, consider whether the strategy is intended for solo or squad play. Some strategies only work in one context.

**Can tier rankings be wrong?**

Tier rankings are community consensus based on observation, not absolute truth. Individual experiences may differ from consensus. Some strategies that rank lower may work better for specific players. Use tier rankings as guidance, not absolute rules.
