The flashlight is Clark Huss rooms's only navigation tool and also its primary vulnerability. Holding the flashlight on provides visual confirmation but exposes your position to Clark. Turning it off preserves battery and reduces detection but limits your visual information. Choosing between flashlight use and audio-only navigation is one of the game's most important strategic decisions.

This guide compares flashlight-on and flashlight-off strategies across multiple dimensions, explains the battery management implications, and helps you decide which approach to use in different situations. You will also learn about hybrid approaches that combine the benefits of both.

## Flashlight-On Strategy

The flashlight-on strategy means using the flashlight frequently for visual confirmation. Veterans use it selectively rather than continuously, but new players often default to sustained flashlight use.

### **Flashlight-on characteristics:**

| Aspect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Visual confirmation | High |
| Battery consumption | Fast |
| Clark detection | High |
| Navigation speed | Faster |
| Confidence level | Higher for new players |
| Best for | Unfamiliar corridors, junction decisions |

The flashlight-on strategy trades battery and stealth for visual information. It works best when you are exploring unfamiliar areas.

## Flashlight-Off Strategy

The flashlight-off strategy means navigating primarily through audio cues, using the flashlight only at critical decision points. This is the veteran approach.

### **Flashlight-off characteristics:**

| Aspect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Visual confirmation | Minimal |
| Battery consumption | Slow |
| Clark detection | Low |
| Navigation speed | Slower |
| Confidence level | Requires audio mastery |
| Best for | Clark proximity, late game |

The flashlight-off strategy preserves battery and reduces Clark detection. It works best for players with strong audio skills and during high-threat situations.

## Click Discipline Fundamentals

Click discipline means using the flashlight as discrete clicks rather than sustained beams. This is the foundation of effective flashlight strategy.

**Click discipline levels:**

| Level | Click Pattern | Battery Cost | Information Value |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------------------|
| 1 | Single click (0.3 sec) | 1% | Corner check |
| 2 | Double click (0.6 sec) | 3% | Corridor scan |
| 3 | Triple click (1 sec) | 6% | Full room reveal |
| 4 | Sustained hold (3+ sec) | 12%+ | Deep exploration |

Veterans default to Level 1 and 2 clicks. Level 3 is used sparingly. Level 4 is reserved for specific situations.

## When To Use Sustained Flashlight

Sustained flashlight use is justified only in specific situations. Outside these moments, click discipline is always superior.

**Justified sustained use:**

1. First entry into an unexplored room — full layout reveal
2. Clark confirmation — hold on his silhouette to verify location
3. Rescue operation — sustained light to navigate while carrying
4. Final extraction approach — maximum visibility for exit identification
5. Item verification — items have small hitboxes, sustained confirms contact

Outside these five situations, sustained flashlight is wasteful and dangerous.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which strategy is better for new players?**

Flashlight-on with click discipline is better for new players because it provides visual confirmation while building audio skills. Pure flashlight-off is too advanced initially.

**Can I switch strategies mid-run?**

Yes. The hybrid approach requires switching strategies fluidly based on situation. Veterans switch constantly.

**What is the best strategy for late game?**

Flashlight-off is best for late game because battery is critical and Clark is more alert. Audio-only navigation maximizes remaining battery.

**Does flashlight use affect Clark's behavior?**

Yes. Sustained flashlight increases Clark's detection range significantly. Click discipline minimizes this effect.

**Should I ever use sustained flashlight?**

Yes, in five specific situations: unexplored room entry, Clark confirmation, rescue operations, final extraction, and item verification.
