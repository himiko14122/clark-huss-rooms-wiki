**What is the most common mistake when starting out?**

The most common mistake is treating Clark Huss rooms like a typical horror game where visual cues dominate. Audio-first design means new players must develop listening skills that other games do not require. This learning curve catches many experienced horror game players who rely on visual instincts they have built elsewhere.

**How do experienced players differ from beginners?**

Experienced players move more deliberately, use flashlight less frequently, communicate more clearly in squads, and recognize Clark audio tells within fractions of a second. Beginners tend to sprint, hold flashlight continuously, panic during chases, and miss critical audio cues. The gap closes with deliberate practice over 5 — 10 sessions.

**What is the biggest improvement you can make quickly?**

Improving flashlight discipline provides the fastest improvement for most players. Switching from continuous flashlight use to click-only mode extends battery life dramatically. The extended battery life means more visual information is available when needed, improving overall performance.

**How important is audio equipment really?**

Audio equipment is critical. Built-in laptop speakers and TV speakers cannot provide the directional audio cues the game is designed around. A $30 pair of wired earbuds dramatically improves gameplay compared to built-in speakers. The investment pays off immediately.

**Should I focus on solo or squad play?**

Focus on the mode that matches your goals. Solo play builds individual skills faster. Squad play provides longer survival times and social engagement. Most veterans play both modes to develop well-rounded abilities. Choose based on your priorities and available time.

**What role does luck play in survival?**

Luck affects run outcomes significantly because of procedural generation. A well-prepared player in an unlucky layout may die, while a less prepared player in a favorable layout may survive. Focus on skill development to minimize the role of luck over time. Long-term success requires consistency across many runs.

**How do I avoid burnout?**

Burnout is real for active players. Take breaks between sessions, play other games, and vary your squadmates. Set realistic goals for each session. If you find yourself frustrated, step away for a day or two. Returning fresh improves both enjoyment and performance.

**What is the best way to learn advanced techniques?**

Study veteran gameplay through community resources. Watch experienced players on streaming platforms. Read detailed guides on specific topics. Practice specific techniques in isolation before applying them in real runs. Deliberate learning accelerates skill development compared to passive gameplay.
